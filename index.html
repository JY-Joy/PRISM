Abstract

While modern text-to-image (T2I) models excel at generating images from intricate prompts, they struggle to capture the key details when the inputs are descriptive paragraphs. This limitation stems from the prevalence of concise captions that shape their training distribution.

We propose PRISM, a compositional approach that enables pre-trained T2I models to process long sequence inputs. PRISM uses a lightweight module to extract constituent representations from the long prompts. The T2I model makes independent noise predictions for each component, and their outputs are merged into a single denoising step using energy-based conjunction.

We evaluate PRISM across a wide range of model architectures, demonstrating comparable performances to models fine-tuned on the same training data. Furthermore, PRISM demonstrates superior generalization, outperforming baseline models by 7.4% on prompts over 500 tokens.

Motivation

Modern text-to-image models are trained on concise captions, limiting their ability to process long descriptive paragraphs. Simply fine-tuning on longer prompts fails to generalize to unseen prompt lengths.

PRISM addresses this by reframing long-text-to-image generation as a compositional task. Rather than training a monolithic model to interpret an entire paragraph, we decompose the input into semantic components that pre-trained T2I models can readily understand, then compose their outputs to synthesize the final image.

Method

PRISM consists of three main components: (1) a lightweight decomposition module that learns to extract constituent representations from long-prompt encodings; (2) multiple independent noise predictions for each component; and (3) energy-based conjunction to merge outputs into a single noise prediction for coherent scene rendering.

Results

Superior Generalization

PRISM demonstrates superior generalization to out-of-distribution prompt lengths. Unlike fine-tuning approaches that degrade performance on longer prompts beyond their training distribution, PRISM's compositional nature enables it to handle arbitrarily long inputs.

In our experiments, PRISM consistently outperforms baseline models across all prompt length ranges, with particularly strong gains on challenging long prompts—achieving 7.4% improvement on prompts over 500 tokens. This generalization capability stems from decomposing long inputs into semantic components that pre-trained T2I models can process effectively, regardless of total input length.
